In short

Who this page is for

  • Parents of children in international or bilingual schools who speak English well but are marked down on reading, writing or comprehension.
  • Spark support is built around five specific skills: reading, writing, vocabulary, comprehension and communication.
  • Plans are shaped around your child's actual school — its curriculum, report cards and homework — not a generic syllabus.

Where Students Get Stuck

International and bilingual schools move fast into academic demands: independent reading, structured writing, textbook comprehension and class discussion — all in English, often alongside a second curriculum language. A child who is socially fluent can still be a year or more behind the reading and writing level their school assumes. That gap rarely closes on its own; it needs targeted, structured support.

How Spark Support Works

A Plan Built Around Your Child, Not a Syllabus

Every student starts with a free Spark Readiness Assessment that identifies exactly where support is needed. From there, small classes of 4–6 students — taught with the same Structured Literacy and phonics foundation behind every Spark program — target those specific gaps, with 20–25 individual feedback moments in every 45-minute lesson.

Students then move along the Phonics → IB English → ESL → IELTS pathway at their own pace, so support keeps making sense as they grow.

Skills We Strengthen

Reading

Decoding, fluency and comprehension, so students read independently at or above grade level.

Writing

Sentence structure, paragraph organization and essay skills that match school expectations.

Vocabulary

Academic and subject-specific words, not just everyday conversational vocabulary.

Comprehension

Understanding, inferring and evaluating what's read — the skill exams and textbooks assume.

Communication

Presenting ideas clearly in discussion and written work — a skill international schools grade directly.

Working Alongside Your Child's School

Spark isn't a replacement for school — it's built to work alongside it. We shape each plan around the language your child's specific curriculum and report cards actually use, whether that's the IB Learner Profile, British curriculum IGCSE terms, or an American-curriculum reading level. Parents are welcome to share school feedback so we can target it directly.

Common Questions

FAQs About English Support

Will Spark coordinate with my child's school?

We don't have formal partnerships with schools, but we shape each student's plan around their actual school curriculum, report-card language and homework, and are glad to review school feedback with parents.

My child is already "good at English." Do they need support?

Often, yes. Many students who speak fluently still lack the academic vocabulary, sentence structure or reading stamina their school curriculum expects. A free assessment shows exactly where the gaps are, if any.

Does English Support replace my child's school English class?

No. Spark works alongside your child's school, reinforcing and building the specific skills — reading, writing, vocabulary, comprehension and communication — that classroom time often can't cover one-on-one.
