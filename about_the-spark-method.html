How We Teach

The Framework Behind Every Lesson

Every parent wants to know the same thing: what actually helps children make meaningful progress? At Spark, the answer is a consistent framework — not luck, talent, or simply more hours in class.

  1. Home
  2. /About
  3. /The Spark Method

That is why every lesson, program and learning pathway at Spark is guided by our S.P.A.R.K. framework — five essential elements that support long-term literacy, language development and academic success. Together, they help students become confident readers, capable writers, effective communicators and independent learners.

In short

The five letters, at a glance

  • S — Systematic Instruction: skills taught in a logical, building sequence.
  • P — Personalized Learning: small classes so feedback fits each child.
  • A — Academic Language: the vocabulary and structures school actually requires.
  • R — Reading Mastery: decoding, fluency and comprehension taught explicitly.
  • K — Knowledge Building: background knowledge that makes new texts easier to understand.

Learning Built Step by Step

Students learn best when new knowledge is introduced in a logical sequence. Rather than teaching isolated skills or jumping randomly between topics, Spark follows structured learning pathways that build understanding gradually over time. Each new skill connects to previous learning, so students strengthen existing knowledge while developing new abilities.

For example: sounds are taught before words. Words are taught before sentences. Sentences are taught before paragraphs. Paragraphs are taught before essays.

This systematic approach reduces confusion, improves retention, and helps students build confidence as they experience success at every stage.

What Parents Notice

Students become less reliant on guessing and develop a stronger understanding of how language works.

Feedback Fitted to Each Child

No two students struggle in the same place. In a class of 4–6, a teacher can hear every child read aloud, see every child's sentence, and correct the specific mistake that student is making — not a generic one. That is the difference between a lesson delivered at a class and a lesson taught to each student in it.

Spark students receive 20–25 individual feedback moments in a single 45-minute lesson, compared with 2–3 in a typical large class. Pace, questions and practice adjust to the child, not the other way around.

What Parents Notice

Children get corrected and encouraged in the moment their mistake happens — not days later on a returned worksheet.

The Language School Actually Requires

Conversational English and academic English are different registers. "Academic language" means the vocabulary, sentence structures and text types international-school subjects demand — comparing evidence, structuring an argument, using precise rather than everyday words. Spark teaches this explicitly rather than assuming it will be picked up through exposure alone.

Students practice academic language across reading, writing and discussion, so it becomes usable — not just recognizable.

What Parents Notice

Children start using more precise, subject-specific language in both their writing and their explanations at home.

The Skill Every Subject Depends On

Reading is the foundation every other academic subject is built on. Spark teaches decoding, fluency and comprehension as explicit, practiced skills — not something a child either "has" or doesn't. Structured Literacy and systematic phonics come first, so students decode rather than guess from pictures or word shapes.

As decoding becomes automatic, attention frees up for comprehension — understanding, inferring and evaluating what has been read.

What Parents Notice

Children read more, more often, and start choosing books slightly above their comfort level on their own.

What You Already Know Shapes What You Understand

Comprehension depends heavily on background knowledge — a reader who already knows something about a topic understands new text about it far more easily. Spark lessons build general and academic knowledge alongside literacy skills, so students bring context to unfamiliar passages instead of decoding words without grasping meaning.

This is especially important for international-school students moving between subjects, curricula and, at times, languages.

What Parents Notice

Children make more connections between what they read and what they already know from school or daily life.

How the Five Work Together

No single letter of S.P.A.R.K. works in isolation. Systematic Instruction sets the sequence; Personalized Learning makes sure each child actually keeps up with it; Academic Language and Reading Mastery are the two skill pillars being built; and Knowledge Building is what lets a child apply all of it to a brand-new text. Every Spark program — Phonics, ESL, IB English and IELTS Success — is this same framework, adjusted by level.

Common Questions

FAQs About the Spark Method

Is the S.P.A.R.K. framework used in every lesson?

Yes. Every program at Spark, from Phonics through IELTS Success, is planned and taught through the same five S.P.A.R.K. principles — only the specific content changes by level.

Is this method evidence-based?

Yes. Structured Literacy, systematic phonics, and explicit academic vocabulary instruction are well-supported approaches in reading science, adapted here for international-school English learners.

How is this different from a typical English class?

Most conversational English classes optimize for speaking confidence. The Spark Method optimizes for reading, writing and academic language ability, delivered in small classes where every principle can actually be applied to each child.
