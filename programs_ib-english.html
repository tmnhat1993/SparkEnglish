In short

What this program covers

  • Who it's for: students in IB, IGCSE, AP and other international-curriculum schools who speak English well but need academic literacy.
  • The problem it solves: the gap between conversational fluency and the academic reading, writing and analysis school requires.
  • The approach: a six-level pathway from paragraph writing to advanced literary analysis.
  • The outcome: confident readers, capable writers and independent thinkers — not just better grades.

Why Academic English Matters

Many students appear fluent in English but struggle when faced with academic tasks. They can hold conversations comfortably, yet find it difficult to analyze a text, explain an author's purpose, support an opinion with evidence, interpret unfamiliar ideas, or organize complex ideas in writing.

Academic English requires a different set of skills than conversational English — and those skills support success across English, Humanities, Science, the IB Programme and future examination pathways. Academic English is the language of learning.

The Spark Approach

A Structured Pathway to Academic Literacy

The IB English Program follows a structured pathway that develops literacy and academic communication over time. Students read critically, discuss ideas thoughtfully, write regularly, analyze evidence, build academic vocabulary and reflect on their own thinking.

The program combines guided instruction, writing instruction, vocabulary development, literary analysis, discussion and debate, critical thinking and research skills. Each level builds on the last and prepares students for increasingly demanding academic tasks.

Program Levels

Building Strong Writing Foundations

Students at this stage build the confidence and skills needed for academic writing. Students learn to write clear sentences, organize a paragraph around one main idea, and use basic grammar accurately in context.

By the end of this level: students write organized paragraphs with a clear main idea and supporting detail.

Structured Paragraph Writing

Students learn topic sentences, supporting detail and linking words, alongside close reading of short texts for meaning and detail.

By the end of this level: students produce multi-paragraph writing with a clear, logical structure.

Essay Foundations

Students learn the introduction–body–conclusion essay structure, compare ideas across two texts, and expand their academic vocabulary.

By the end of this level: students write a complete, structured essay on a familiar topic.

Analytical Reading & Writing

Students identify an author's purpose and tone, analyze literary devices, and construct evidence-based arguments in writing.

By the end of this level: students write analytical essays that use textual evidence to support their points.

Critical Analysis & Independent Thought

Students evaluate multiple perspectives on a text, undertake extended literary analysis, and take part in structured debate and discussion.

By the end of this level: students write independent critical essays on more complex texts.

Advanced Academic Communication

Students refine sophisticated argumentation, nuanced literary interpretation and exam-style analytical writing, preparing directly for IGCSE English and pre-DP work.

By the end of this level: students produce exam-ready academic writing and analysis.

Common Questions

FAQs

What is the difference between ESL and IB English?

ESL builds general English communication across all four skills. IB English assumes conversational fluency and focuses specifically on academic reading, analytical writing and literary study.

Is this suitable for international school students?

Yes — it's designed specifically for students in IB, IGCSE, AP and other international-curriculum schools who need stronger academic literacy skills.

Does the program improve essay writing?

Yes. Structured essay writing — from paragraph organization through analytical, evidence-based essays — is a core focus at every level.

Does it support IB and IGCSE students specifically?

Yes. The program's later levels are built around the academic writing and literary analysis skills both the IB Programme and IGCSE English assess.

My child speaks English fluently. Will this program still help?

Almost certainly. Conversational fluency and academic English are different skills — most students in this program already speak comfortably but need explicit teaching in academic reading and writing.

Does the program include literature study?

Yes. Literary analysis — literary devices, author's purpose and interpretation — is taught alongside non-fiction academic reading and writing.
